Beyond the job board: finding the original creative employer.

Why official career pages matter, and how to use a studio directory without confusing discovery with live hiring.

S/O
Studio OpeningsNov 24, 2026 · 2 min read
Share by email

The employer is the source of truth

A directory helps you discover practices you might not know. It cannot guarantee that a vacancy is still available when you visit. Follow the original employer recruitment page to check the role, location, eligibility and application requirements.

Different studios recruit in different ways

Some practices use a dedicated careers page, while others link to an applicant tracking system. Further has a current careers route under its own name; Framestore links from its careers site to its recruitment board. The Whitney Museum directs applicants through its job-postings page. These examples come from Studio Openings' reviewed career-source directory, not an assertion of active openings.

Build a studio shortlist

Choose employers by the type of work, the role you want and the places where you can work. Review their official projects and recruitment instructions. Keep track of the date you checked the source rather than assuming a historical vacancy remains active.

Use discovery and verification together

Start with the Studio Openings directory and follow each individual profile to the official source. If the job board has related results, inspect those as a separate discovery step. An employer profile is not a paid advertisement or a guarantee of a partnership.
