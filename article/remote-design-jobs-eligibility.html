Remote does not always mean worldwide.

How to read location restrictions, time zones and employment arrangements in creative job advertisements.

s
Studio OpeningsNov 24, 2026 · 1 min read
Share by email

Start with the employer's location language

A remote job can still require residence in a particular country or region. A company might employ workers only where it has payroll infrastructure, or require proximity to clients or regular team meetings. The company's headquarters is not proof of where you can work.

Distinguish the work arrangement from eligibility

Remote describes where the work takes place. Eligibility describes who may apply or where they may be based. Hybrid generally requires some office attendance, but the frequency is employer-specific. Look for explicit country lists, office requirements, travel expectations and time-zone overlap.

Read ambiguous listings conservatively

If a vacancy says remote but gives only an office city, do not assume that the city is the sole eligible location or that every country is eligible. Ask the employer which jurisdictions are supported. A useful question is: Can a person residing in my country apply, and would this be an employee or contractor arrangement?

Compare the practical terms

Before accepting an offer, confirm working hours, currency, pay period, equipment responsibilities and the applicable contract. A contractor arrangement may shift tax, insurance and unpaid leave responsibilities to you. Obtain appropriate professional advice for cross-border employment questions.
