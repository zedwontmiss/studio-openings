A checklist for evaluating creative job openings

Assess role scope, portfolio expectations, work location and the original application source before applying.

s
Studio OpeningsNov 24, 2026 · 2 min read
Share by email

A job title alone rarely tells you what a creative role involves. Before sending an application, use the original employer posting to check the following details.

1. Read the actual responsibilities

Distinguish concept development from production, management and technical coordination. An architecture role may emphasize documentation or BIM rather than concept design. A creative director may own strategy and team leadership, while a graphic designer may focus on execution. Match the responsibilities to the work you want to do.

2. Check location and eligibility separately

Remote does not necessarily mean worldwide. Confirm permitted countries, time zones, work authorization, office attendance and any relocation requirements. If eligibility is missing, ask rather than assuming it.

3. Match your portfolio to the role

Choose relevant examples that explain your contribution, constraints and outcomes. For architecture, technical drawings and project stages may matter alongside visual work. For motion or VFX, a focused reel may be more relevant than a general portfolio.

4. Compare compensation and contract scope

Check currency, pay period, employment type and whether a figure is gross or net. A freelance day rate cannot be directly compared with annual employee pay without accounting for unpaid time and benefits.

5. Verify the original vacancy

Use the employer's authoritative career page or application system to confirm the opening is current. A directory entry can become outdated between checks. Avoid sending unnecessary sensitive documents to an unverified recipient.
