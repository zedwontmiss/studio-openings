When a design studio becomes a platform.

Tadaima’s Copenhagen retail and design practice offers a way to think about creative independence beyond client commissions.

FI
Falah IbrahimNov 26, 2026 · 2 min read
Share by email

What if the most consequential project of a young design practice is not a commission, but the institution it builds for itself? Copenhagen-based Tadaima offers a useful case through which to consider that question.

According to its own account, Tadaima began in 2022 with an online shop, opened a showroom in 2023, presented an exhibition and a collaboration with Fredericia in 2025, and opened its Apartment Store in 2026. The practice combines its own design work with a selection of other makers’ work.

The platform is the project

For a creative practice, a commissioned project generally begins with someone else’s brief. An owned platform introduces another possibility: the practice can decide which subjects to explore, whose work to present and how to communicate with an audience. A shop, journal or exhibition programme can become a framework for those decisions rather than merely an outlet for finished objects.

Tadaima also describes its journal as a way of contextualising the work of designers. The interesting relationship here is between making, selecting and explaining. Each activity can reinforce the others, offering audiences more than a catalogue of products.

Independence has costs

An owned platform can offer greater control, but it is not a shortcut to financial independence. Retail can introduce inventory and premises costs; publishing takes sustained editorial effort; exhibitions require production and coordination. These are general trade-offs, not a claim about Tadaima’s finances.

For younger practices, the useful question may be smaller: what can we build that creates an ongoing relationship with people who care about our work? A digital publication, a limited-edition object or a recurring gathering may be a starting point. The appropriate scale depends on the resources of the team.

A different measure of relevance

Creative influence need not be measured only by the names on a client list. An independent initiative can also create a context in which other designers are discovered and ideas circulate. That is one reason Studio Openings is interested in the projects and platforms that practices initiate themselves.

This is an independent editorial interpretation of Tadaima’s published account, not an interview, site visit, financial assessment or sponsored feature. Primary source: Tadaima, About Us, https://tadaimacph.com/studio/about-us (accessed 26 November 2026).
